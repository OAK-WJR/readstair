ReadStair Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-23

ReadStair marks the words you don’t know in books you scan, open or write. In short:

  • No account, no ads, no tracking. No email address is ever asked for, and a name only if you publish a book. Nothing is sold.
  • Your books stay on your device. Your word list also syncs through your own iCloud, which the developer cannot see.
  • ReadStair goes online for four things: Library books, a book you open by its link, reading voices you ask for, and publishing a book if you choose to.
  • The developer gets only a book you publish, the id of a book you report and, as every website does, your device’s IP address.
  • Camera: to scan pages and, if you turn it on, for Eye Tracking, which runs on your device and saves no picture.
  • Children: ReadStair is for all ages. Readers under 13 cannot publish, and nothing personal is collected from them.

On your device

ReadStair keeps these in its own storage on your device, and in your device backups if you make them:

  • The books you scan, open or get from Library: their pages, their text and, for some, the file itself
  • Books you write, with their cover pictures
  • Your word list, with the sentence, book and page where you first met each word, and your vocabulary level
  • Your settings, the libraries you added, the reading voices you downloaded and the books you reported
  • For a book you published, a key in the device’s Keychain (see Publishing on readstair.com)

In your iCloud

If iCloud is on for ReadStair, your word list and vocabulary level sync between your devices through your private iCloud database. For each word that is the word, whether you know it, how you marked it, how common it is, when it changed and the one sentence where you first met it. Nothing else from your books is synced. Only you can open this database. The developer cannot, and Apple’s privacy policy covers how Apple stores it.

When ReadStair goes online

  • The ReadStair library comes from readstair.com: its lists, its covers and the books you tap. While you have a book that a reader wrote, ReadStair asks readstair.com, each time you open ReadStair or come back to it, for a new version of the book or a take-down.
  • Report Book sends readstair.com only the book’s id, and nothing about you. The report is deleted 30 days after a person has dealt with it.
  • Reading voices are downloaded from voices.readstair.com if you ask for them, and ReadStair checks there which voices it offers. They speak on your device.
  • Other libraries you choose. Project Gutenberg, for readers 13 and over, sends its list and books, and receives the words you search for when you tap Search. A shared Google Drive folder you add by its link is read from Google, without your Google account. Their own privacy policies apply.
  • Open Link in the + menu on Books gets from Google the PDF, EPUB or slideshow whose Google Drive link you paste, without your Google account. Google’s privacy policy applies.

Each request carries what apps normally send: your device’s IP address, ReadStair’s name and version, your iOS version and your language settings. Beyond what this page says a feature sends, it carries no account, no device identifier, no age and nothing from your books or your word list. ReadStair’s own code never reads or stores the IP address. Its hosting provider uses the address only to deliver files, to protect the sites and to count visits, and keeps a sample of recent requests, with their addresses, for up to 31 days, which the developer can see. The address is never used to contact you, to recognise you, to build a profile or for ads. ReadStair saves no cookies.

Publishing on readstair.com

If you are 13 or over, you can publish a book you wrote. Anyone can then read it on readstair.com and in Library.

  • What is sent: the title, the first name or pen name you type, the story, the cover, the ages you tick, whether AI helped, where the cover picture came from and the terms you agreed to. A cover photo is saved again from its pixels first, so its location and camera details are gone.
  • Review. A person at ReadStair reads every book before it is public, and declines one that holds personal information such as a full name, a school or a photo of a real child. No AI reads it.
  • What is public: the story, the cover, the title, the name you typed, the ages, the AI mark, when it was sent, and counts of views and hearts. Search engines can find it.
  • Taking it back. You can take back a book that is waiting. If iOS says you are under 18, or doesn’t say, you can remove a published book at once: ReadStair tells readstair.com that you are under 18, and it stores only that the author removed the book. If you are 18 or over, you can ask, and a person at ReadStair decides. Copies someone already saved cannot be taken back.
  • What is kept afterwards. When a book is declined, taken back or removed, its story and cover are deleted at once, its title and the name you typed after 30 days, and a record with no content after one year. The hosting provider’s backups can hold them up to 30 days longer. A book or a report held as possibly illegal is kept, hidden, for up to one year.
  • Your key. A random key in this device’s Keychain is the only link between you and a book you sent. While you have one, ReadStair uses it to ask readstair.com, each time you open ReadStair or come back to it, where the book is now. Deleting the book in ReadStair deletes the key, and this device can then no longer remove the book.

Eye Tracking

Eye Tracking is a switch in a book’s More menu. It is off until you turn it on, and it turns itself off when you leave the book or ReadStair, or when the camera can’t see your face for a minute.

  • While it is on, ReadStair uses the front camera and iOS’s face tracking to work out, on your device, where on the page you are looking. An underlined word you look at for about a second opens its word card, and when you tap a word ReadStair takes it that you are looking at that word, to follow your eyes better. That is its only use: never to recognise you, and never for ads, analytics or a profile.
  • No picture or video is saved, and no record of where on the screen you looked is kept. The positions of your face and eyes, and what Eye Tracking works out from them, exist only in memory until you leave the book. They are never stored, put in iCloud, sent to the developer or shared with anyone. ReadStair’s log on your device notes only how well Eye Tracking was set, as distances, and which word cards a look opened.
  • To keep ReadStair from using the camera at all, turn the camera off for ReadStair in Settings > Privacy & Security > Camera.

Camera, photos and files

  • Camera: only while you scan pages, and the front camera only while Eye Tracking is on.
  • Photos and files: iOS shows its own picker, and ReadStair receives only what you pick.
  • ReadStair never uses the microphone, your location or your contacts, and shows no notifications.

Apple features

Text recognition, translation, the iOS voices and Apple Intelligence’s word explanations run on your device. iOS may download translation languages from Apple. Image Playground, if you use it for a cover, is Apple’s own window: ReadStair gives it ideas from your title and the first 1,000 characters of your story. Apple’s privacy policy covers these. If you turned on Share With App Developers in iOS Settings, Apple may give the developer crash reports and usage statistics.

Children and parents

  • Age. ReadStair never asks your age. It asks iOS for your age range, which you or a parent can decline to share. The answer is used on your device, to choose which books Library shows and whether Publish is offered, and for removing a published book (see Taking it back). ReadStair keeps no age in its own storage: if you decline, or iOS can’t share a range, the device remembers only that ReadStair asked, so it doesn’t ask again by itself. When iOS shares a range, ReadStair’s log notes the age group Library uses (4+, 9+ or 13+). Share Age Range, in Library’s title menu or in Publish, asks again.
  • Under 13. ReadStair collects no name, email address or other personal information from children under 13, and they cannot publish anything for others to see. Its sites receive only the IP address described above, used only to run them.
  • Parents can ask what ReadStair holds about a child, or have a child’s book removed: see Contact.

Sharing

Nothing here leaves your device unless you send it yourself, through iOS’s share sheet:

  • Share makes a PDF of a book you wrote or scanned, with its title, cover and the author name you typed.
  • Save a Copy makes a file of your word list: each word, what you chose for it, its sentence and book title, and your level. Get My Words Back reads a file you pick and adds its words to your list.
  • Share Log makes a text file of what the app did, for finding problems: its version, your iOS version and device model, times, how well Eye Tracking was set and which word cards it opened. It holds no page text, book titles, words or file names.

Deleting your data

  • Delete a book in ReadStair to remove its pages, text and files. Words you saved from it stay in your word list.
  • Delete ReadStair to remove everything it stored on the device. Keys of published books can stay in the Keychain until the device is erased.
  • To delete your word list from iCloud, open Settings, tap your name, then iCloud, open your iCloud storage, choose ReadStair and delete its data.
  • To remove a book you published, see Publishing on readstair.com.

Versions and changes

This page describes the current version of ReadStair. Version 1.0 has no Library, no publishing, no downloaded voices and no Eye Tracking, and never contacts readstair.com: only the parts about your device, your iCloud, the camera for scanning, Apple features and deleting apply to it.

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here with a new date. ReadStair does not track you across apps or websites, so a Do Not Track signal changes nothing.
